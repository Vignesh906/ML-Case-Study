🔬 Machine Learning Methodology: Resolving the "Accuracy Paradox" in Multimodal Delay Prediction

1. Why Naive Models Collapse on Majority Classes In public transit and commercial flight operations, the majority of trips run On Time. In our test set, {{ "{:.1f}%".format(metrics.class_base_rates['POOLED']['ON TIME'] if metrics.class_base_rates and 'POOLED' in metrics.class_base_rates else 71.3) }} of all pooled observations are On Time, {{ "{:.1f}%".format(metrics.class_base_rates['POOLED']['MODERATELY DELAYED'] if metrics.class_base_rates and 'POOLED' in metrics.class_base_rates else 21.8) }} are Moderately Delayed, and {{ "{:.1f}%".format(metrics.class_base_rates['POOLED']['SEVERELY DELAYED'] if metrics.class_base_rates and 'POOLED' in metrics.class_base_rates else 7.0) }} are Severely Delayed. A naive classifier predicting "On Time" for every record mechanically achieves high accuracy while achieving zero recall on delays and a collapsed Macro F1. High raw accuracy alone is an uninformative metric in imbalanced transit.
2. Why Macro F1 Governs Deployment Unlike weighted metrics or raw accuracy that reward ignoring rare classes, Macro-F1 computes the unweighted arithmetic mean of F1 across all three classes: Macro F1 = (F1_OnTime + F1_Moderate + F1_Severe) / 3. Our active models use tuned validation thresholds and calibrated class posteriors, ensuring balanced decision boundaries across minority delay regimes.
3. Severe Delay Detectability and Coherent Hurdle Modeling Severe delays (> 20 min) represent high operational risk but low prevalence (~5–7% in transit, ~15% in aviation). Standard squared-error regression underestimates extreme delay peaks due to regression-to-the-mean. Our architecture resolves this via a Coherent Two-Part Hurdle Model: Part 1 predicts $P(\text{delay} > 0)$, Part 2 predicts $\mathbb{E}[\text{delay} \mid \text{delayed}]$, and quantile regressors estimate median P50 and 90th-percentile risk (P90) with verified ~90% test coverage.
